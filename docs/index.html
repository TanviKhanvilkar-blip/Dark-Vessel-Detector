SYSTEM OVERVIEW

Ships the radar sees that nobody is tracking

Radar satellites see ships whether or not they announce themselves. AIS is what ships broadcast about themselves. This system compares the two for each processed Sentinel-1 pass and lists the ships that appear on radar with no matching AIS signal.

High confidence = no AIS ship nearby although the AIS feed was healthy and the target is within receiver range, and it is not at a spot where something was also seen on another pass. Everything else is low confidence: the data cannot support calling it dark (for example Mumbai, where no AIS was available for the pass at all).

Pipeline

01
Radar image
Sentinel-1 sees every large ship, day or night, through cloud.
02
Propose
CFAR marks every target much brighter than the sea around it; land is masked.
03 · NEURAL NETWORK
ShipCNN
Our neural network looks at each target and gives P(ship): ship, or pylon, turbine, buoy, clutter?
04
Align AIS
Each ship's last AIS report is moved along its course to the exact image time.
05
Match
The Hungarian algorithm pairs radar targets with AIS ships within a speed-aware radius.
06
Flag
Unmatched ships become candidates, labelled high or low confidence by what the data can support.

What runs by itself. AIS is recorded continuously. Every 15 minutes the system checks the Sentinel-1 catalogue; any new pass that covers at least 65% of a sector and was taken while the recorder was running is downloaded, run through the whole pipeline including ShipCNN, and published here. Passes from before the recorder started were processed with the same pipeline using historical AIS. Read before trusting a flag

"Not publicly tracked" is not "switched off". Small boats often carry no public AIS, free AIS comes from coastal receivers with limited range, and ships under about 40 m are not reliably visible to the satellite. Low-confidence flags are the ones the data cannot support: beyond receiver range, during an AIS feed gap, or with no AIS recorded.
