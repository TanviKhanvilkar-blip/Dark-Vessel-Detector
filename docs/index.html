SYSTEM OVERVIEW

Ships the radar sees that nobody is tracking

Radar satellites see ships whether or not they announce themselves. AIS is what ships broadcast about themselves. This system compares the two after every Sentinel-1 pass and lists the ships that appear on radar with no matching AIS signal.

Pipeline

01
Radar image
Sentinel-1 sees every large ship, day or night, through cloud.
02
Detect

03
Align AIS
Each ship's last AIS report is moved along its course to the exact image time.
04
Match
The Hungarian algorithm pairs radar targets with AIS ships within a speed-aware radius.
05
Flag
Unmatched targets become candidates, with an honest confidence label.

Read before trusting a flag

"Not publicly tracked" is not "switched off". Small boats often carry no public AIS, free AIS comes from coastal receivers with limited range, and ships under about 40 m are not reliably visible to the satellite. Low-confidence flags are the ones the data cannot support: beyond receiver range, during an AIS feed gap, or with no AIS recorded.
